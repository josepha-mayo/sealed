Why sealed scoring and receipt integrity are separate problems

Arcium computes the score. A bad aggregator can still duplicate or omit valid score receipts afterward. Sealed checks both the computation’s captured result accounts and the complete receipt set. MPC confidentiality depends on its committee; the capture’s consistency does not independently establish its chain origin.

verify a ceiling card — proof of a negative

the anti-claim — a sealed-ceiling/v1 card (chain ceiling <model> --snapshot mints them) proves a negative: “no finalized run of this model EVER scored above N%”. Claim cards prove existence — show the receipt. A ceiling card proves nonexistence, possible only because the snapshot is an exhaustive account dump: the card lists EVERY subject run and the page re-derives that set itself from the decoded accounts — a hidden argmax run dies at completeness, a phantom run dies at binding, a shaved number dies at the argmax replay. The primitive a regulator or deployer actually wants: an enforceable upper bound on demonstrated capability. Same checks as chain ceiling --verify.
every card is sha256-pinned in this bundle’s MANIFEST — llama-3.2-1b's saved ceiling is 3.1%; the stale-artifact case is the separate openai run

verify the circuit card — the MPC itself as evidence

the referee is infrastructure, not a server — a sealed-circuit/v1 card (chain circuit --prove --snapshot --rpc mints them) binds the six Arcium computation-definition accounts, the MXE, the Arcium cluster, and the callback SignPda to THIS deployment. Every other card binds what the MPC wrote; this one binds the MPC: each compdef address re-derives in-page as [ComputationDefinitionAccount, sealedPid, u32le(sha256(name)[:4])] under the deployed Arcium program Arcj82pX…, and the SignPda re-derives as a sealed-program PDA that must equal the account sitting in the snapshot. The card also pins sha256 of the actual account bytes — the six raw-circuit bytecode PDAs [ComputationDefinitionRaw, compdef, u8] re-derive, and every recorded hash must match the committed arcium-infra.json dump, including the cluster account's committee BLS key. A renamed circuit lands on a different address; a fabricated one can't exist; swapped bytecode fails its hash. Same checks as chain circuit --verify.

verify the ledger census — the nothing-up-my-sleeve card

every negative claim in this bundle — capability ceilings, leaderboard completeness, the anomalies sweep — rests on one assumption: the snapshot is the WHOLE account set, not a cherry-picked subset. A sealed-ledger/v1 card (chain ledger --prove mints it) is the census: per-program account totals, per-kind discriminator counts, and a sha256 over the sorted pubkey set — all recomputed in-page from the raw bundle bytes. Hide one account and the count lies; swap the set and the hash dies. Honest scope: the census proves the dump is enumerable — a judge can re-derive the same set independently with getProgramAccounts; it doesn't pretend the chain holds nothing else, only that THIS dump discloses every account it claims to. Same checks as chain ledger --verify.
